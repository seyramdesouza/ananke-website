Aseye Tamakloe is a filmmaker, film educator, and cultural activist whose work merges cinema with advocacy, memory, and placemaking. With nearly two decades of experience in Ghana's film and television industries and beyond, she has become a significant figure in shaping African cinematic landscapes.

She has served as a co-programmer for Film Africa Festival, London's largest celebration of African and African diaspora cinema, organised by the Royal African Society, and as the Festival Director for the European Film Festival Ghana. She is also a juror for the Feature Narrative category at Philadelphia's renowned BlackStar Film Festival. She is a board member of the National Film Authority (NFA) in her home country, Ghana.

Her practice focuses on creating and protecting inclusive platforms, which is why she established NDIVA Women's Film Festival, an initiative that showcases, preserves, and celebrates work created by, for, and about women.

Her acclaimed documentary, "When Women Speak" continues to be screened worldwide. Aseye Tamakloe is also a PhD candidate at the Institute of African Studies, University of Ghana. Her research interests are in the fields of African Cinemas, African Urbanities, African Literature, Popular Culture and Gender studies.
